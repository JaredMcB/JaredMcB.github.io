Equations, inequalities, graphs, and the main families of functions: linear, polynomial, rational, exponential, logarithmic, and trigonometric.

## About the course

College Algebra is designed for students with a solid background in high school algebra and provides the basis for further mathematical study. I teach it in two formats.

**In person.** For each section of the textbook students get a reading guide, and we work through it together in class. The guides are meant to help students think carefully about the material and keep organized notes. Practice comes through online homework, there are regular lesson quizzes, and three midterm "Celebrations of Knowledge" and a final measure how far everyone has come.

**Asynchronous online.** The same course, with the same textbook and the same standards, done on the student's own schedule. It is built from about forty lessons, each organized around a short list of numbered learning objectives. For each lesson there is a Lesson Guide and a video in which I work through that guide, objective by objective, with questions embedded along the way. Students then practice in the online homework system and upload their handwritten work so I can see how they actually worked.

**Textbook:** [*Algebra and Trigonometry 2e*](https://openstax.org/details/books/algebra-and-trigonometry-2e) from OpenStax, free online.

**Prerequisites:** Strong background in high school algebra, MAT 105, or permission of the instructor.

## Helpful resources

- [Desmos graphing calculator](https://www.desmos.com/calculator). Free. Graph any function we study and drag sliders to see how its parameters change it.
- [Khan Academy: College Algebra](https://www.khanacademy.org/math/college-algebra). Extra explanations and practice problems for nearly every topic in the course.
- [Paul's Online Math Notes](https://tutorial.math.lamar.edu/). Clear written notes with many worked examples; see the Algebra section.
